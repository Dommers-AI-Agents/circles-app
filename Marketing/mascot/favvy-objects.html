How they're made

One color per part, no pauses, no paint

Every study splits into a few single-color prints that lock together with keyed pegs and a drop of glue, so the finished object is one solid piece. The lit eyes are glossy black inserts with tiny rose dots (or a pair of 3 mm LEDs on a coin cell, if you want them to really glow).

  1. 1
    Body printed upside down on its flat top, so the walls lean in as they rise. No supports, about 2 hours in one color.
  2. 2
    Lid or tray: a walnut blank or wood PLA. A 40 mm recess holds the 38 mm NFC sticker with the 1.5 in QR label over it.
  3. 3
    Stem and foot, with two 1 in steel washers set in the foot, so a phone tap never tips it.
  4. 4
    Face: a glossy black visor insert with two rose eye dots, or two small black pins for the quieter studies.
Recommendation

Start with Bow, with Halo's lit visor

Bow leans toward the customer with a walnut tray for a face, a gesture people read instantly as "welcome, tap here". It's the most elegant to look at and the most natural to use, because the phone lands flat on the tag. If you want the quietest, most Furbo-like option, Halo is the safe second.
