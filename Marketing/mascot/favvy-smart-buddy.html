Favvy, plugged in

A mug-sized Favvy that sits by the register, plugged in and on Wi-Fi. Its face is a small round screen, so it reacts when someone taps, shows the store's specials, and can do more than an NFC sticker can.

What sparked it: the Furbo Mini. It's a friendly white pod with a wood top that people happily keep on a shelf. Favvy can borrow that shape and put its heart on top.

My pick to build first

Favvy Glow

about 12 cm tallparts about $15–25USB-C power
  • A living face. The screen shows Puff's expressions: "Hi there" when idle, "Points!" when someone taps, sleepy after closing.
  • The store's own messages. Today's special, a coupon that ends soon, "47 locals love this place".
  • Tap to earn, as now. The NFC tag sits inside the pod, so the existing register link keeps working.
  • A beacon. iPhones with FavCircles notice it even with the app closed, so the visit is that store and not a drive-by. Stores get a real count of app users who came in, with no camera.

Inside: an ESP32-S3 chip with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, a 1.28-inch round screen, an NFC tag, and a 3D-printed shell.

Favvy Cam

about 14 cm tallparts about $35–60camera points at the counter
  • Scans the customer's code. They hold up their FavCircles code. It works on Android and phones without NFC, and it confirms the visit, so points aren't purely on trust.
  • Scans the receipt. Points for what was actually spent, like delivery-receipt points but in person.
  • Selfie with Favvy. A photo only when the customer presses the button, sent to their phone as a Moment or share card.
  • Everything in Glow: the screen face, NFC and beacon.

The camera only reads codes and receipts on the device. Nothing is recorded or streamed, and there's no microphone.

Favvy Deluxe

wider baseparts about $70–110add-ons to Glow or Cam
  • A speaker. A chime and a short line, like "Welcome back!" or "Reward unlocked!", when someone taps. Staff and customers both hear that it worked.
  • A little ticket printer. It prints a "come back Thursday" coupon or a prize someone just won. Something physical to take home.

The printer needs paper rolls and adds the most cost and support. I'd treat it as a later add-on.

Window Favvy

25–30 cm signparts about $40–80faces the street
  • A sign more than a counter piece. A light-up Favvy in the Neon style, with a live count that updates from the store's page.
  • Tap through the glass. The window NFC tag sits behind it, so passers-by can save the store.
  • A beacon for the sidewalk. People walking past get "you're near a place your friends love".

Things to get right

  • Privacy. No microphone. The camera only reads codes, on the device, and never records. Skip people-counting cameras; the beacon gives that number.
  • Wi-Fi setup. This is where these products usually go wrong. Setup should happen in the app, the way Furbo does it.
  • Updates and security. A device on store Wi-Fi needs signed updates sent from our server.
  • Who pays. Fits the Business pack (included, or a deposit). Free NFC stickers stay the entry level.

Getting to a prototype

  • Off-the-shelf boards (M5Stack, ESP32-S3-Box, about $30–50) inside a 3D-printed Favvy shell.
  • Pre-certified radio modules avoid most of the FCC approval work.
  • The app and server side reuse what exists: register tag links, rewards, store messages and the Locals tab.
  • A working Glow prototype is realistic in weeks, not months.
