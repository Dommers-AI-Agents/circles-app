The FavCircles symbol, made solid. A rounded rose heart sits inside a ring, held at its tip as if it were set in jewellery, on a sturdy base. Printing it on your P1S

Four parts, each one color

Each part prints separately in a single filament, so there are no AMS color swaps (the reason the multi-color figure took 12 hours). Every part lies flat on the plate with no supports. Times and weights are from Bambu Studio at 0.16 mm Fine with 3 walls.

Assembly

About ten minutes, no tools

The heart is printed as two halves because a shape rounded on both sides can't lie flat in one piece without supports. The engraved design on the front half gets filled with a Posca paint pen.

  1. 1
    Write and lock the NFC sticker with the store's Points link, turn the base over and stick it to the ceiling of the round pocket underneath. It sits 1.7 mm under the QR label.
  2. 2
    Close the heart. Two short pieces of 1.75 mm filament go into the matching holes and line the halves up; a thin line of super glue (or 5-minute epoxy) seals them. Sand the seam lightly if you like.
  3. 3
    Set the heart in the ring. The heart's tip ends in a printed stem (half on each heart half) that slides into a socket at the bottom of the ring. Add a drop of glue.
  4. 4
    Seat the ring in the base. It drops into an 8 mm slot. Glue it, add four rubber bumpers underneath, and stick the QR label in its recess, right over the tag.
Why it works by a register

It's a recognisable logo rather than a toy, so it suits a wine bar as well as a bakery. "Tap or scan the circle" is a one-line instruction staff can say without thinking. A tap presses down on the wide base instead of pushing the top, so it can't tip, and none of the thin parts stick out to snap off.
